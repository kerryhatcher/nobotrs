In Georgia, LLCs are generally subject to the same taxes as other businesses. This includes:Federal income tax: LLCs are taxed at the federal level on their profits, just like other businesses. The tax rate will depend on the LLC's income and the number of owners (known as "members").State income tax: Georgia does not have a state income tax, so LLCs are not required to pay state income tax on their profits.Sales tax: If your LLC sells goods or services that are subject to sales tax, you'll need to collect and remit the tax to the state.Self-employment tax: If you are the only owner of your LLC and you receive income from the LLC, you'll need to pay self-employment tax on that income. This tax is used to fund programs such as Social Security and Medicare.Employment taxes: If your LLC has employees, you'll need to pay employment taxes, including federal income tax withholding, Social Security tax, and Medicare tax.It's important to note that this is just a general overview of the taxes that may apply to your LLC. You should consult with a tax professional or the Internal Revenue Service (IRS) for more specific information on your tax obligations.
