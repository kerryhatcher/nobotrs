The Persian Wars were a series of conflicts fought between the Greek city-states and the Persian Empire in the 5th century BCE. The wars took place primarily in the region of Greece, although some battles also occurred in other parts of the Persian Empire, including present-day Turkey and Iraq. The main causes of the wars were the expansionist policies of the Persian Empire, which sought to extend its control over the Greek city-states, and the desire of the Greeks to defend their independence and way of life. The wars were characterized by a number of significant battles, including the Battle of Marathon, the Battle of Thermopylae, and the Battle of Plataea, which are still remembered and studied today for their historical and cultural significance.
