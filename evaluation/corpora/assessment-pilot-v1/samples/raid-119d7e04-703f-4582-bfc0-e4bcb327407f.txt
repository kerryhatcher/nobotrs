We consider stochastic particle systems on weighted graphs, called parameter- dependent random oscillator models (PROMs). In contrast to PROMs with fixed parameters, oscillators undergo small perturbations in each time step according to their neighborhood structure and position on the graph; such dynamics are well described by Fokker–Planck equations (FPE) . Under mild assumptions, these FPE turn out related to Ott–Antonsen (OA) type partial differential equations that admit attractive, traveling wave solutions giving rise to stable, self-organized states consisting of stationary phases or phase clusters moving along graph edges. Examples of biological interest include neural models like the Kuramoto model capturing synchronization among brain cell populations coupled via inhibitory and excitatory synapses as well as chemical kinetics like the Belousov–Zhabotinsky reaction describing spatiotemporal patterns during catalytic oxidation reactions. Our analysis provides efficient methodology based on a combination of numerical studies supported by a mathematical justification using optimal transport theory and statistical mechanics for ODE’s
