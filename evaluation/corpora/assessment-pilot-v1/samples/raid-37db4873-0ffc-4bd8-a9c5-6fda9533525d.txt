> We investigate to what extent the structure of different proposals for effective field theory descriptions (``matter models'') in quantum gravity can be tested against each other. To this end we identify and study several features that all known consistent models of this kind share, starting by deriving an estimate that upper bounds universal properties at a given energy scale within quantum gravity. This has important implications concerning universality classes that follow directly from renormalization group methods without specifying concrete model choices or approximations based on them. As a second step, we derive constraints that are related to the existence condition of classical observables compatible with quantum space-time singularities under the assumptions of general covariance -- such as a unique causal past/future structure in Minkowski space -- and stability/applicability conditions imposed by nonperturbative consistency requirements derived explicitly using large distance expansions. In particular, our results show that ``conventional'' standard models like perturbation theory around flat $\Lambda$CDM have no chance to survive these tests while other candidate constructions such as loop quantum cosmology do. However, even though stringent, it turns out nevertheless possible for viable candidates not only to avoid strong constraints stemming from one side of the set up but also satisfy the remaining ones -- a finding which may indicate that a rather broad class of asymptotically secure predictions could emerge from unified quantum theories of nature if sufficiently powerful criteria towards testing their internal self-consistency shall apply in their frameworks.
