Abstract Chained variables are used in many fields ranging from mathematics to machine learning and computer graphics. Common chaining examples include Markov chain Monte Carlo, LSTM neural networks and variational autoencoders. Despite their prevalence, not much is known about chains beyond low-dimensional or stochastic settings. In this work we study such properties on infinite-sized spaces where it is hard to directly apply classical tools from geometry and analysis. We show that some popular operations (namely product and scaling) preserve chains while summation does not always commute with them; yet we prove that products do form vector spaces under appropriate conditions. By studying convexity, the property which allows us to use powerful tools like duality, we provide fundamental building blocks towards analyzing and designing new algorithms for high-dimensional chains.
