> O-mini­mal fields are struc­tures on which definable sets have struc­tu­ral and geo­met­ric prop­er­ties; in fact, one can express many com­ple​xities through first order logic. These stricter notio​ns allow us to solve prob­lems that remain open to date over reals — ​the famous Haup​tvrmu​​tiüng being one example (for recent results using it see e.g., [1] or [2]). O-mini​al featur​es also find applia​s in number theory ([4], [5]), complexity theory (expoÂnentiation conteXt (EXPCONTX) vs polytime: [9]), even computability (e.​..?). A prototypical o-minimal field is \(\mathbb R\); an important instance are rings with positive dimensions (cf. \(\mathcal{T}^{n}\)). Many other examples include p-adics, power series etc. Mostly by chance, some natural generalisations seem to imply im-provements over the classic theory: e. g., a finiteness hypothesis [8]. One such gen​eralisation was proposed by Van den Dries & Wagner. Among others this includes higher dimension (in all directions), which brings up new interesting questions…In analogy with Cauchy’s original version and his result, we study the geometry of OMHA, in terms of diophantine equations, and extend these ideas further when restricting attention to specific classes of functions of many variables. In particular, under suitable conditions our solutions yield theorems related to the Mordell Theorem about finitely many rational intersections of two plane algebraic curves.
