> We consider Bäcklund transformations (BTs) as a mathematical tool for finding connections between integrable equations or as an exact analytical construction, which generates solutions to non-linear differential equations by means of successive non-trivial solution pairs satisfying BT relations (equilibrium points). In general our study aims at characterizing different types of BT pairs (pure/singular), explaining all possible linkings with integrability criteria including Darboux transformation and reduction procedures (RPs) related to Lax-operators, symmetries in Lie algebras etc., elucidating the interplay of BT with non-local conserved quantities (NCQs) via the inverse scattering problem (ISP) approach, exploring new ideas on local NCQs based upon the notion that BT provides a sort of nonabelian Fourier analysis (NAFA), developing also some new BT schemes like e.g., the "modified BT", or establishing links of integrable systems families associated to finite gauge theories. Our main results are based on a classification scheme for any pair of BT generating functions given within the algebraic variety of all formal series expressed in terms of one single function variable x only, which leads us directly to a classication of all BTH's. For example, we illustrate this approach towards several integrable systems, among them a two-dimensional KdV system together with its discrete analogue. It goes without saying that there exist lots more examples both in classical physics as well as in modern theoretical or mathematical physics where similar reasoning could be applied. Of course these constructions can be handled not exclusively at the level outlined above but rather should - especially if further developed theoretically - pave ways into applications requiring tools in the field of numerical analysis, too. More precisely, a better understanding of BTs will finally enable us to make progress particularly at the interface between numerics and mathematics; this becomes obvious by referring to a recent BT algorithm providing highly efficient solutions for constructing invariant manifolds of dynamical chaotic flows near equilibrium/coherent structures or just computing periodic orbits/isolated 3D homoclinic tangencies - all the latter ones being key features in chaos synchronization problems nowadays used intensly for e.g. medical diagnostics purpose etc..
