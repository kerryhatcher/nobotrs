Porous semiconductor has been extensively studied because it can exhibit different properties compared with
its bulk counterpart, including higher surface ratio and high catalytic activity under potential bias. In this report, we study the electrostatic doping and the nanoelectronics mechanism in sulfide layers
of MoS2. We find that upon exposing to potassium salt solution (K+), Mo atoms are gradually replaced by potassium cations in the thin film along zigzag directions due to the formation of K+ vacancies and adsorbate complexes on the S edges. As the surface defects concentration increases by increasing KCl dose at room temperature (RT) or by decreasing the film thickness at RT or -90 C, our first principles calculation reveals that not only hole density (noh), but also charge mobility(μcH) dramatically improve with thickening of space between Mo row lines perpendicular to the pore walls, reaching up to two orders magnitude enhancement over that of pristine system when KCl concentration increases >0.2M. However, Kdoping breaks the structural integrity for the single layered MoS2, resulting in delamination failure in experimental samples with μm thickness at RT without any external stimulus, thus limiting its application to low frequency devices (e.g., thermoelectrics). Our results highlight both advantages and limitations of such new type nanoengineering to tune the performance of MoS2 based electronic devices as well as to fabricate large area Mo/MoS2 heterojunction structure for future studies.
