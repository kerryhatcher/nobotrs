See the abstract and PDF below: --------------------- Crystal structures are usually solved based on Fourier maps calculated after Xray or neutron diffraction measurements, which require enormous experimental efforts to obtain high quality data under controlled conditions, often also assuming no structural dynamics are present and neglecting thermal vibrations and lattice defects. Here we solve crystals using electron diffraction (ED) patterns that can be more easily obtained with scanning transmission electron microscope measurements due to lower sample damage, shorter diffusion time of carriers within materials and possibility of operating close to ambient temperature. Furthermore, ED requires less scattering volume which significantly benefits for nanostructures whose atoms are closer together than macroscopic crystal structure samples for X-rays or neutrons. ED has already been used to verify known atomic structures but mostly neglecting disorder. To overcome this limitation and take into account dynamical effects of disordered systems, here we developed the first complete theory for atomic reconstruction of disorder in real space beyond the harmonic approximation. Using a genetic mutation algorithm called GODAN, we demonstrate that our theory can directly construct perfect lattices from disordered systems with both atomic positions and local order parameters determined at the same time. For example, starting with an observed ED pattern of graphite containing a small fraction of amorphous carbon we extract the atomic positions of each different type of atom as well identify how much of each species exists locally around every position. In addition, our method determines atomic distributions without knowing any symmetry breaking operations like mirror plains because it simply reproduces the observed ED peak positions by varying atomic structures until the best overall fit to all peaks is achieved by including local order parameters necessary for solving the phase problem to determine absolute phases needed by the structure determination. Because atomic level correlations appear naturally, this information must be included in such models, whereas they may not necessarily produce reliable results when ignoring them like traditional methods [Figures show model-simulation agreement between model simulated EDS and experimentally measured ones]
