Here we present an overview of various results obtained in recent years with neutral atoms trapped by optical lattices, aiming both to give a panoramic view and discuss open questions. We review four examples and provide an analysis that should prove useful in further interplay between experiment and theory. In particular we have tested the validity of Wannier's semiclassical approximation when computing effective kinetic energy terms up to second order of the Berry phase. Experiments concerning nonadiabatic geometries are also highlighted here, providing new tests of predictions stemming from Berry phases and illustrating novel phenomena like negative mobility or dissipationless flow under electric field. A key example shows how to achieve coherent control over quantum states on a plane, leading to unexpected topological effects such as transience via quantized charge pumping through vortex holes in the condensate plane. This highlights the potential of this methodology beyond mere geometric effects. More generally, these works show progressively closer links between non-abelian topology (which was first uncovered through theoretical studies using adiabatic methods) and experimental realization of concrete observables that could be measured within flux-braiding protocols.
