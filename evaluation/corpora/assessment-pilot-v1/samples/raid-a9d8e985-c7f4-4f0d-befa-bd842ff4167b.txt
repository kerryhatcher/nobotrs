In this work we develop a novel generalization of Kendall's social force model, and demonstrate that when an evacuee experiences local variation from its preferred path towards a destination at speed V0(t), as opposed to uniformly slowdown (due to density or road conditions) when navigating along said optimal trajectory (as assumed by existing theory), it will perform a continuous maneuver around obstacles, rather than performing discrete changes of direction (the so-called steering movements). We then show experimentally using actual camera footage from the Newport Earthquake Evacuation Trial in Los Angeles California, where 456 participants were required to follow navigation directions provided on tablets whilst walking inside a building during the simulation of an earthquake, that if subjects make these naturalistic micro-maneuvers then they can successfully reach their intended destinations while maintaining safe interpersonal distance despite any density encountered during normal locomotion and crowd panic/panic-like behavior such as sudden surges toward exits and collisions at doorways.
