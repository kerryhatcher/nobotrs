We study an extended version of the celebrated Jaynes–Cummings model (JCM), comprising two one-mode quantum oscillators of different frequencies $\omega_\alpha$ ($\alpha=1,2$) interacting via both direct coupling and virtual exchanges of a bosonic quantum field. These interactions are mediated by an atom which is initially prepared in an internal state with superposition coefficients $z_{1(2)}$, being either symmetric or antisymmetric relative to that corresponding to the groundstate $|g\rangle$. We obtain a general analytical expression valid for arbitrary $R \equiv z_2/z_1$, the ratio between the interaction strengths induced by these states onto each cavity mode; such formulation includes as special cases several previously considered variants of JCM dynamics. For small values of $R$, we observe spin-polarized (symmetry breaking) states where Rindler type singularites arise when at least one of the modes becomes overdamped due to strong decay [cfr.$|\xi|^4|\kappa_a|<\min(\omega^2,\kappa)-2\mu N|\omega_\alpha-\omega_b|$;$\alpha=1,2$], so it cannot be excited. By making use also of recently established results on the behaviour near the critical point $(\Omega=0)$ [for further details see Poletti et al., Phys. Rev. Lett, Vol. 97 Issue 6, pp. 53803 (2006); doi:Dai10.1103/PhysRevLett.97.065380] we find that for large values of the parameter $r$, symmetry (spin-) fluctuations become vanishingly small leading eventually to spontaneous macroscopic polarization across very long distances. Moreover, through numerical integration of stochastic differential equations, analogous behaviour is verified to have its correspondence (with higher precision) in the master equation derived under a Markovian assumption.
