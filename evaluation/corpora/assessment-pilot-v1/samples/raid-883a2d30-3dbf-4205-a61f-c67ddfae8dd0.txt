The advent of diffusion weighted imaging (DWI) methods capable of characterizing multiple tissue compartments promises increased diagnostic potential via multicontrast DWI imaging protocols such as Multiple Shell Quantification Sequences (MSQS). However, this advantage must be weighed against significantly reduced SNR in these techniques due to their acquisition time requirements or spatial coherence limitations across high b value shells used to estimate diffusivity for each given compartment. Recent research into the Generalize q- Sampled Image Reconstruction (GRAPPA)-type reconstruction technique known Generalized Q-sampling Methodology (GAMER) has overcome several important limitations associated with other nonparallax acceleration schemes by allowing direct recovery from a single sampling scheme that combines measurements at arbitrary q factors without the need for complicated models describing data-collection geometry. In this study, an adaptation of GEMAR, called GENuM-D, is introduced in order to recover signal from MnSQI acquisitions using only generalized encoding (GE) encoded k-spaces collected on a single subject shell in contrast to previously proposed approaches which use predefined priori knowledge about expected image content. This work demonstrates how it's possible to reduce the number of samples required per spatial location when acquiring data across compressed bands while increasing analytic accessibility. It also presents initial performance measures suggesting improvement over state of the art methods for performing this type of analysis under noise conditions mimicking practical MR imaging scenarios including phantoms modeled after human brain structures imaged at T2^*=3sec^-1 and T2
 =80msec respectively; however further testing will likely yield more concrete conclusions concerning both accuracy relative to ground truth solutions as well qualitative comparisons regarding reconstructed images.
