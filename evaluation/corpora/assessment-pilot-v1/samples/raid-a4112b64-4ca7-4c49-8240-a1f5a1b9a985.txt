 This work suggests that AM can be used to distinguish between gravitational waves and other astrophysical sources. (RMS)
The authors are Drs. Christopher R. Berry, John Voyten, Dmitri Frolov, William Hajdu and David J. Shoemaker at MIT Haystack Observatory and Dr. Lia Medvedova-Pasichnyk at MIT Kavli Institute. This manuscript was submitted in April 2019; however some changes were made on July 31st. The summary has not been updated to reflect those edits.
Abstract
As we approach observational sensitivities where gravitational-wave detection will coincide with a number of background or disturbances other than those resulting purely from gravity it becomes increasingly important to develop new methods of distinguishing these signals through different observable characteristics. In this Letter, we first describe amplitude modulations within an existing data set taken from LIGO's second observing run that have signatures indicative of orbital motion but deviate significantly from pure Keplerian binaries due to interactions within the system and show that even considering higher order nonlinear effects do not properly produce the observed properties. It is then argued that if this residual signal correlates in time with a candidate binary merger event as seen by two detectors, then it may be interpreted as evidence consistent with a binary interaction source. Lastly, it is quantified how effectively both simulated intermodulation artifacts originating in coalescent inspiral simulations as well as true binary black hole orbital motions could mimic the characteristics of the anomalous phenomena previously described. Through various Monte Carlo tests, our model results suggest that only systems in eccentric and highly inclined orbits would pass thresholds comparable to what had been measured while remaining physically achievable configurations. Due to these findings, analysis of any potential candidates should include checks against such systematic errors before accepting them as genuine detections of alternative physics events
