[Begin Text] IMPACT OF ELECTRODE DENSITY OF STATES ON TRANSPORT THROUGH PYRIDINE-LINKED SINGLE MOLECULE JUNCTIONS Author(s): Lakshmi Gopala K. Pradeep, Michael Mora-Flores* and F. Cenk Yal?n† Corresponding Author Email address: email hidden; JavaScript is required Abstract Electron conduction through organic molecular wires has been observed by many experimental techniques over the last two decades. For understanding charge flow in low dimensional conductive systems, tunneling theory developed as an alternative to the Drude band transport model. In spite of some successes, however, there are still numerous experiments that demonstrate results which cannot be explained using this framework (Gammon et al., 1998). To rationalize such observations we have proposed a new approach where electron-phonon coupling controls quantum transport at low temperatures and is mediated through fluctuations about phononic eigenmodes (Pradeep & Yal?, 2006, Physica B 350/351, pp 152-155). To study how the electronic properties may develop when the molecules connect to metallic contacts with varying densities of states, we present here Monte Carlo simulations of ballistic tunnelling based upon the Anderson tight binding Hamiltonian, for monolayers consisting of 50 molecules connected tip to tail. We find a small electrostatic repulsion between adjacent atoms provides a natural mechanism to reduce the electronic transmission. Moreover, decreasing the number of electrons available within each terminal produces a significant increase in the transmission coefficient, regardless of the potential seen by the central junction. These results shed some light onto our previous studies concerning low bias charge modulation.
We note additional simulations will follow soon where the effect of thermal noise is included.* Universidad Autonoma de Tamaulipas. † Universitas Muhammadiyah Malang [End Text]
