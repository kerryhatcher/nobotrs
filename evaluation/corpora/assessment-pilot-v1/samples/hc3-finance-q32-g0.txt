Self-insuring means that you are taking on the risk of financial loss yourself, rather than transferring that risk to an insurance company through a policy. It can be a good idea to self-insure for smaller losses that you can easily afford to pay out of pocket, but it is generally not a good idea to self-insure for large losses or disasters, such as a fire or a natural disaster, as the financial impact of these events can be significant and difficult to recover from.Renter's insurance is a type of insurance that provides coverage for your personal property and liability in the event of a loss, such as theft, fire, or water damage. It can also provide coverage for additional living expenses if you are unable to live in your rental due to a covered loss. Renter's insurance is typically relatively inexpensive and can provide valuable protection for your personal property and financial well-being.In general, it is a good idea to have some level of renter's insurance to protect against potential losses, even if you are able to self-insure for smaller losses. It is important to carefully consider your individual circumstances and the level of coverage that is appropriate for your needs. You may want to consider speaking with an insurance agent or financial advisor to help you determine the right amount of coverage for your situation.
