ADRs (American Depository Receipts) are securities that represent ownership in the shares of a foreign company that are traded on a U.S. stock exchange. They are traded in U.S. dollars and are priced in the same way as other securities listed on the exchange. The price of an ADR is determined by supply and demand for the underlying shares on the foreign stock exchange, as well as other factors such as the financial performance and outlook of the company, market conditions, and investor sentiment.The value of an ADR may also be affected by changes in the exchange rate between the U.S. dollar and the currency in which the underlying shares are traded. If the value of the foreign currency declines relative to the U.S. dollar, the value of the ADR may also decline, even if the value of the underlying shares remains unchanged.In general, the price of an ADR will be closely tied to the price of the underlying shares, although there may be some differences due to factors such as the fees associated with holding and trading the ADRs, and any differences in the trading hours or liquidity of the two markets.
