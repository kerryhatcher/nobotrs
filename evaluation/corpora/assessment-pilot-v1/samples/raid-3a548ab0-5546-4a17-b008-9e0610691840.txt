2018.63 (2019), arXiv 1807.08470 [physics.soc-ph]. Abstract. This article makes progress towards answering an old question which seems to go back, at least in part, as far as 1901 and has been answered for specific tournaments but never, so far as we know, generally: Given a tournament with N teams, who will win? There are many interesting cases where this problem has not yet been solved; if someone can prove that Team A always wins when there are more than three teams, or determine how large of a team advantage is required for Team B to be favored over Team C given that these two strategies have won all previous encounters, they shall have our admiration! In this work a general strategy selection algorithm based on Bayesian reasoning is employed to examine the relative probabilities that different teams would theoretically win under varying assumptions about their efficiencies and strengths as perceived by sports analysts. Our results seem to indicate that small variations across even very modest ranges of expected probability values for various outcomes may change who is expected to prevail, making precise estimates hard to pin down unless a greater amount of data regarding the capabilities of individual competitor teams is available upon which such predictions may be based.
Incidentally I think one reason why few people look into it and/or expect it to remain unexplorable is because they're simply using a linear approach while expecting exponential returns; whereas the process requires multiples to generate any positive output, meaning only a fractal pattern can get you real close to what you really want most, namely 1-of-N chances instead of merely ~1% odds...so long as your input parameters aren't also randomized according to unknown distributions within themselves throughout every iteration otherwise too much risk becomes involved once again -- perhaps explaining why people might shy away from pursuing better strategies altogether despite obvious mathematical benefits waiting around somewhere here & nowhere else!
