We propose new metrics for field of view (FOV) in far and nearfield optical beamforming. Optical wavefront sensors combined with digital signal processing allow us to obtain resolution beyond the diffraction limit by synthesizing images at subwavelength scales using phase measurement over several spatial samples taken away from the focal plane. We derive expressions that capture the dependence on each parameter required to measure multiple fields within an FOV, which include: the transverse size of single measurements; minimum sampling density imposed by the Nyquist–Shannon theorem, and the number of spatial degrees of freedom captured per individual data sample. For applications where high spatial coherence may be employed we show how this can extend lateral resolution limits and provide more accurate aberration maps when measured across different pupil positions while still maintaining small numbers of spatial DOF and fast collection times that render our technique suitable even for microscopic bioimaging and other sensitive experimental setups such as photonic crystal systems operating under the light emission regime. We conclude through examples in both farfield [1, 2] and superresolution nearfield [3, 4] scenarios, presenting design rules that are practical even for nonideal hardware. In addition it should also prove instructive towards the optimal operation of any given experimental setup seeking higher resolution capabilities than would otherwise allowed by the inherent Rayleigh or Abbe diffraction limits.
