The Declaration and Resolves of the First Continental Congress were a series of statements adopted by the First Continental Congress during its meeting in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania in 1774. The Declaration and Resolves were a response to the increasing tensions between the American colonies and the British government, and were intended to serve as a unifying document for the colonies as they sought to address their grievances and defend their rights.\n\nThe Declaration and Resolves outlined the colonies' complaints against the British government, including the imposition of taxes and other measures without the consent of the colonists, the abuse of power by the British military and civil authorities in the colonies, and the interference with the colonies' trade and commerce. The document also affirmed the colonies' rights as English subjects, including the right to be governed by laws made by their own elected representatives, the right to trial by jury, and the right to the protection of their property.\n\nThe Declaration and Resolves called for the colonies to unite in their efforts to address these grievances, and to resist any attempts by the British government to impose further measures that would infringe on the colonists' rights. The document also called on the colonies to form committees of correspondence to keep in touch with one another, and to maintain a united front in their efforts to defend their rights and liberties.
