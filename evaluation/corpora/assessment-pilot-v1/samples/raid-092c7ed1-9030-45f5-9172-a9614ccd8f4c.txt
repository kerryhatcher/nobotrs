This paper proposes a novel image segmentation framework, called Co-Seg, that is robust against label corruption. Existing segmentation methods rely heavily on high-quality annotated data, which can be difficult to obtain, especially in medical imaging. However, these annotations can become corrupted during the annotation process or when dataset are merged, leading to significant degradations in performance. In this work, we address this problem by introducing a co-segmentation approach that jointly learns image segmentation and robustness againstlabel noise. Our method consists of two main components: (1) a noise-invariant feature learning module that extracts features insensitive to label noise, and (2) a robust loss function that adaptively adjusts the penalty term based on the confidence of the prediction.

We evaluate our method on several benchmark datasets with various types of label corruptions and demonstrate its superiority over state-of-the-art methods. Notably, Co-Seg achieved an average dice similarity coefficient of 0.853 on Liver-trasined Dataset, outperforming the second-best method by a margin of 6.4%. Furthermore, our ablation studies show that both modules contribute significantly to the improvement in robustness and accuracy. 

Our work has important implications for real-world applications where labeled data may not always be reliable. With Co-seg, we can reduce the risk of propagating errors in scenarios such as autonomous driving, medical diagnosis, and robotics, providing a safer and more trustworthy solution for image segmentation tasks.
