We present an extensive review related to beam steering techniques which can be used with different types of electromagnetic metasurfaces such as reflectarray antennas or meta-lens arrays. It has been demonstrated that these devices have the potential of enabling reconfigurable systems suitable for various emerging applications within communication networks including dynamic spectrum access and multibeaming where conventional solutions cannot satisfy key requirements as agility, tunability bandwidth or form factor. Furthermore, they also allow the design of smaller size miniaturized passive lens feeds based on planar structures. In addition, they are compatible with other new technologies like MEMS and Liquid crystal in order to reach wider angular coverage. To achieve this goal several beam control techniques are investigated ranging from traditional ones already implemented in space communications such as Frequency, Phase or Time domain methods up to more exotic alternatives coming from materials science and photonics area such as Metasheet theory, Photonic bandgap metasheets or Multi-frequency approach as well. However no systematic comparison among them is provided due to their diversity. Therefore the focus is set on reviewing all beam control principles and techniques for active microwave phased array antennas using electrically large substrates that can provide arbitrary wavefront control capability. Conclusions about each method limitations and feasibility of actual implementations will be finally derived afterwards with regards to both antenna sizing parameters and fabrication technology.
