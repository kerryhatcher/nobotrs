Excerpt follows--and note, this report doesn't seem to include ion heating or noise due to resonant molecular excitation (which seems important) and it does not yet deal with nonlinearity-induced ion heating as discussed at: http://www.fizyka.ru/~pavloff/. Ion heating has been found experimentally to be very strong in LSITs with high voltage difference across the trap region (as opposed the low voltage in standard Paul apparatus). This may explain why the LISM experiments at JILA have failed so far.
LISM stands for Light Impact Sympathetic Molecular Surgery ("molecules in motion"), which is designed to perform chemical surgery on living proteins. It requires precise control over individual ions. Such control was recently demonstrated by a UK group using single atoms inside a conventional Paul apparatus (see JILA announcement @ http://info....html ), but no one has done this before with molecules and their potential medical applications are enormous, including better drugs and vaccines (e.g., against HIV / AIDS), customized cancer treatments that employ specific information about each patient, etc,etc,etc. There's tremendous hype around the project, even though it uses only off-the shelf technology readily available worldwide today for use by many groups, most importantly being the recent invention of electric field gradiometers that allow extremely precise manipulation and positioning of trapped ions.
