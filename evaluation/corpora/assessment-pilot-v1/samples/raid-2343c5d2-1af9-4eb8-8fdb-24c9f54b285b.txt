Abstract: In this letter, we demonstrate that the cathodoluminescent (CL) emission spectrum of triangular gold nanostructures depends on their shape and size as well as on excitation parameters; voltage and sample temperature (around room). We show resonance excitations of optical modes with distinct energy when scanning the CL spectra at specific voltages and compare them to those obtained via near field atomic force microscopy measurements performed at the same conditions . Furthermore, applying finite difference time domain method, we calculate the local electric fields distributions around each nanotriangle to support our experimental observations.. By increasing both electron acceleration voltage and CL acquisition temperature, we shift and change significantly several spectral features in the range between 550 nm -1350 nm of these structures revealing their photophysics behaviour as plasmonic mode oscillators. To understand better how one varies with the different experimental variables mentioned above, these variations are compared by considering the influence of dielectric permittivities and temperatures on the electronic densities of states as well as thermal population distribution factors of the metal. All these results allow us to propose an interpretation of the observed photoemission changes in terms of electron transfer mechanisms triggered in the surrounding dielectrics which could explain some discrepancies previously reported on luminescences of noble metal clusters.
