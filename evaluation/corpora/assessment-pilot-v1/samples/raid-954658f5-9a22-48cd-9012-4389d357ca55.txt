(https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.08579)
-------------------------------------------------------
We report on a measurement performed in 2013 investigating $^{24} \mathrm{Mg}( p,\, \alpha ) ^{21} \mathrm{\,Na \,at \,an \,energy \,resonance \,of \,E_r}=1\,\meV$\,. We achieved an unprecedented precision in yield measurements with a statistical uncertainty below $1\%$. This allows us to constrain and test models including two-nucleon transfer processes such as $(\text{(a,\,aa)}\,)\gamma\!\!\nearrow(p,\alpha)$, which may be important ingredients to reproduce astrophysical flow and neutron capture rates. However, we find that none of the models considered can simultaneously provide good agreement with our experimental excitation function with both the measured rates and the nuclear structure properties required input within uncertainties of about $30\%$. Furthermore, all models suffer from too large branching ratios predicted towards excited levels higher than the ground state by at least a factor three, i.e., contradicting experimental data determined via $(d,t)$ reactions.
