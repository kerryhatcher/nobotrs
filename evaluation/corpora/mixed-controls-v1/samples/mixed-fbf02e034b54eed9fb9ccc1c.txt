Uncertainty has very far reaching effects.   Oil is up ~100% since February and down ~40% from it's 52 week high (and down even more on a longer timeline).  It's not exactly a stable investment vehicle and moves a few percent each day on basically nothing.  A lot of securities will be bouncing around for the next couple weeks at least while folks remain uncertain about what the "brexit" will actually mean.

The Brexit, or the United Kingdom's withdrawal from the European Union, is not directly responsible for the fall in crude oil prices. Crude oil prices are determined by a variety of factors, including global supply and demand, political instability in oil-producing countries, and the strength of the US dollar (since oil is priced in dollars).However, the Brexit may have indirectly affected oil prices by affecting the global economy. The UK is a major economic player, and the uncertainty surrounding the Brexit process may have contributed to economic uncertainty and weaker demand for crude oil. Additionally, the Brexit may have had an impact on the value of the British pound, which may have affected the price of oil in pounds.
