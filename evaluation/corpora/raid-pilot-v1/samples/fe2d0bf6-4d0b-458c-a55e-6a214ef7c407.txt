The purpose of this article is to develop a novel unsupervised approach for semantic image segmentation that leverages both Generative Adversarial Networks (GAN) andCLIP, two state-of-the-art methods for style synthesis and vision. Specifically, we propose to use Segmentation-GANs (Seg-GANs), which are GAN models specifically designed for generating realistic images within a specified domain, alongside CLIP, an image embedding model trained on a massive dataset. Our method uses these pre-trained models to create a novel conditional GAN architecture that can be fine-tuned for the task of image segmentation based solely on labeled regions instead of requiring pixel-level labels. Results show that our proposed method achieves comparable performance to supervised learning algorithms while significantly reducing computational demands and training time required, making it a more feasible solution for real-world applications in areas such as medical imaging or autonomous vehicles.
