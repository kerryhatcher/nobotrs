In this coming-of-age novel, we follow the journey of a young girl named Sal as she navigates through the complexities of growing up in the 1960s. Set in a small town in Washington state, Sal's life is filled with the ordinary yet profound moments that define childhood. She is a bright and curious girl who is constantly seeking knowledge and understanding, and her imagination and curiosity often lead her on adventures that take her beyond the confines of her small town.

Sal's life is further complicated by the fact that she has two moons - her real mother and her grandmother, who both live with her. Her grandmother, who is from the Lummi Nation, is a wise and gentle woman who teaches Sal about the traditions and customs of her people. Her real mother, on the other hand, is a troubled woman who struggles with addiction and mental illness. Sal's relationship with her mother is complex and often fraught with tension, as she tries to understand and love her despite her mother's flaws.

As Sal grows older, she begins to explore her own identity and sense of belonging. She is torn between her Lummi heritage and the dominant culture of the white world around her, and she struggles to find her place in both worlds. She also grapples with her own sense of self-worth and self-doubt, as she tries to navigate the challenges of adolescence and find her own path in the world.

Throughout the novel, Sal learns important lessons about love, loss, and the power of family. She discovers the strength and resilience of her grandmother, who teaches her about the beauty and wisdom of her Lummi heritage. She also learns about the importance of forgiveness and understanding, as she tries to heal the rift between herself and her mother.

In the end, Sal emerges as a strong and confident young woman, ready to take on the world and make her own way. She has learned to embrace her identity and her heritage, and she is determined to use her knowledge and experiences to make a positive impact on the world around her.
