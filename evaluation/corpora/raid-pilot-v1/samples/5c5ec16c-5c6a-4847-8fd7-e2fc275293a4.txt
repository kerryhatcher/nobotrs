“Saber-tooth” is a narrative involving a brutal Neanderthal. The characters are those of a Neanderthal community, which has lost its traditions and old ways. Turok, the young ambitious Neanderthal, challenges the customary rule of his chief, and that is the cause of the subsequent conflicts. But when the tribe encounters a saber-toothed tiger, Turok saves them in an unexpected way. By an extraordinary bond with the saber-tooth, whose name is Khalon, he manages to tame it, and use its extraordinary strength and ferocity in hunting expeditions to support the tribe. The second part of the novel shows how Turok and his tribe deal with the difficult and dangerous conditions of the land and the threat of other predators. Turok is the most powerful man in the tribe, and some of the tribe are envious of his power and the fidelity of Khalon. This causes a mutiny against Turok, which leads to a desperate battle that tests the loyalty of Khalon and the ties of the tribe. Turok’s bond with Khalon changes the view of the tribe to the saber-tooth, and they learn to live in harmony with the fiercest animals of the wild. “Saber-tooth” is a stunning exploration of the relationship between humans and animals, tribal politics, and power struggles. It is a poignant reminder of the struggle of our ancestors and their unique relationship with the environment around them.
