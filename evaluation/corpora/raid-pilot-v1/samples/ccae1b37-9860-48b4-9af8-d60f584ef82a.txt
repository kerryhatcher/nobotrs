Inter Extreme Points (IEPs) provide a promising approach to weakly supervised image segmentation. IEPs are points in images that can be computed from a small region around them using geometric properties such as distance and angles, without requiring explicit object detection or pixel-level labeling. In our work, we explore their potential in weakly supervised automatic segmentation by analyzing their structural relationships with local neighbors and other regions within an image.

We show that IEPs possess rich information about object boundaries, shape, texture, and contextual hierarchy, which makes them well suited for weak supervision. However, direct use of IEPs is limited by their low resolution compared with pixels, leading to spatial ambiguity. To address this limitation, we propose a novel framework that constructs intermediate representations between spatial neighborhoods and pixels based on smooth 3-D geodetic paths connecting extreme points across regions with similar geometric structures. Our scheme explicitly exploits spatial continuity, while preserving local geometric relationships among elements in these regions.

Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of intermediate levels of supervisory information using IEPs to improve accuracy for both unsupervised and semi-supervised image segmentation tasks. It also enables visualization of underlying image structure through analysis of IEP cluster connectivity patterns. Overall, our proposed method offers new insights into interpreting spatial hierarchy and integrating nonlocal features at various scales and resolutions for weakly supervised image processing in computer vision applications.
