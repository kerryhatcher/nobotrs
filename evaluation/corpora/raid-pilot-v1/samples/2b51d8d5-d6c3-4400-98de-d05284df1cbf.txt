In this study, a Bayesian dense inverse search algorithm (BDIS) is proposed for real-time stereo matching in minimally invasive surgery. The BDIS algorithm utilizes the prior knowledge of the surgical environment to adaptively refine the search results and improve accuracy. The performance of the algorithm is evaluated using simulated data and demonstrated in vivo on an ex vivo heart specimen using a stereo endoscope system. Results show that the BDIS approach outperforms traditional methods by reducing search time and improving detection sensitivity. Overall, the proposed method holds promise as a valuable tool for enhancing precision during minimally invasive surgeries where accurate image processing is critical.
