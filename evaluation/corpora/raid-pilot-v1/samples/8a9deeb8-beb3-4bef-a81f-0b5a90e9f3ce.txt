Former Arsenal manager Arsène Wenger has offered a lifeline to the Romanian footballer Adrian Mutu, who has had a troubled career on and off the field. Mutu, now forty-one, who played for such clubs as Chelsea, Juventus and Fiorentina, had to deal with his own personal problems and was sacked by the English club in 2004 after he was caught taking cocaine. He was banned for seven months by FIFA and had to pay damages of 140,000 pounds to the English club. However, the former French manager, known for his ability to develop talent, is convinced that Adrian can still play a significant role in the sport. “It is never too late to change one’s destiny,” said the man who ruled Arsenal for a quarter of a century. Wenger was famous for bringing Robert Pires back into the fold after a serious injury to his knee, and stood by Tony Adams while he was battling alcoholism. “Everyone makes mistakes and everyone deserves another chance,” he said. “Adrian has paid a high price for his actions, but it should not define him. He can still have a positive impact on the world of football.” Mutu, who is currently without a club, has not yet responded to Wenger’s gesture. But some of the Romanian’s former colleagues and coaches have joined Wenger in his support, hoping that Adrian will overcome his personal problems and return to the sport he loves. Wenger’s comments come at a time when the mental well-being of footballers is under the spotlight. It is hoped that his gesture will not only give hope to Adrian, but will also stimulate a wider discussion about the kind of support available to players coping with personal problems. Even if Mutu does not decide to return to the game, Wenger’s comments have reignited the debate on second chances.
