In this research, we propose an effective few-shot segmentation approach that combines global and local contrastive learning to achieve outstanding performance. Our method utilizes self-supervision techniques by leveraging two types of contrasts: global contrast which aligns features from different parts in a single image patch, and local contrast which aligns feature maps across patches from nearby regions of different images. By integrating these contrasts simultaneously, our approach can learn robust semantic segmented representations with high discrimination against diverse backgrounds while remaining computationally efficient. We validate our model's efficacy on multiple datasets with limited training data, demonstrating state-of-the-art results and outpacing prior methods in terms of accuracy, tradeoff between speed and quality, and generalization ability across unseen or challenging classes and scenes.
