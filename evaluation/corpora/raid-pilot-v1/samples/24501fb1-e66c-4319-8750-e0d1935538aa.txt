In every heart and soul
Buried deep beneath the land,
There grows a longing wild,
For recognition and command.

The fame that we so crave
That gives us strength to endure,
Is like an amphetamine rave,
A fever with no cure.

It speaks to something primeval,
Inside our very core,
And stokes it up and makes us fervent,
As if we were never poor.

But in its grasp it holds a danger,
As potent as desire can be,
For some will do whatever they must suffer,
To bask in glamour's fleeting glee.

Yet still, there are others who resist,
Whose hearts remain pure at heart,
Who know the true worth of dignity,
And prize their virtues apart.
