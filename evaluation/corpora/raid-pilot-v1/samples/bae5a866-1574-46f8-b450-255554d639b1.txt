The aim of this study is to propose an efficient and generic interactive segmentation framework for correcting mispredictions during clinical evaluation of medical images. The proposed framework uses a combination of deep learning algorithms, including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), attention mechanisms, and region-proposed object detection methods. 
The framework has been designed to be flexible and modular, enabling it to adapt to different types of medical images and specific requirements of individual clinicians. It also incorporates an intuitive user interface that allows clinicians to interactively correct misidentifications made by automated algorithms.
The results of our experiments demonstrate that the proposed framework can significantly improve accuracy and reduce errors in medical image analysis tasks, compared to traditional approaches based on manual validation or fully automated systems. Additionally, the framework's ability to learn from corrected responses provides valuable feedback to machine learning models, improving their performance over time. Overall, the proposed interactive segmentation framework represents a significant advancement in the field of medical imaging and has important implications for improving patient outcomes in areas such as diagnostic medicine, surgical planning, and radiation therapy.
