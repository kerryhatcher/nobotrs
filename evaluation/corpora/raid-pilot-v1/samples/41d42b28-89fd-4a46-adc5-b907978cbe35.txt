‘Angle of Repose’ is a deep and stirring novel, exploring the complexities of love, relationships, and the human spirit’s capacity to survive. It is the story of Lyman Ward, a retired historian who, while suffering from a debilitating bone disease, is working on a biography of his grandparents, Oliver and Susan Ward. The novel moves between the present, as Lyman grapples with his physical limitations and his family problems, and the past, as he meticulously reconstructs his grandparents’ lives through their letters and journals. Oliva, a brilliant engineer, and Susan, a cultivated, artistic East Coaster, settle on the remote frontier of the late nineteenth century. At first filled with hope and love, their marriage gradually succumbs to their clashing ambitions, isolation, and the unrelenting hardships of pioneer life. As Lyman traces their lives, he uncovers the profound sacrifices they made, their unspoken resentments, and the compromises they made: what he calls their ‘angle of repose’. The story of Lyman’s own life begins to parallel his grandparents’ as he deals with his own failing health, the growing estrangement from his son, and his growing love for his carer, Ada. The novel is a beautiful interweaving of the past and the present, as Lyman finds comfort and understanding in his grandparents’ story, finally coming to terms with his own ‘angle of repose’. It is a testament to the power of history and personal reflection in shaping our understanding of love and loss.
