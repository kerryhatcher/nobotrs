The tennis great Andre Agassi is worried about the coming Australian Open, which starts in Melbourne on January 20, citing the recent rise in swine flu cases in the city as a possible threat to the tournament. "Everyone is going through hard times, but the situation in Melbourne is particularly disturbing," Agassi told the BBC. "The health of players, officials and fans must be a priority." Melbourne, which hosts the first Grand Slam of the year in January, has seen a significant increase in swine flu cases in recent weeks, which has led to stricter restrictions and led to questions about the success of the international event. Agassi, who retired in 2006, also referred to the possible impact on the players' preparation for the tournament. "This uncertainty is difficult for the players," he said. "They need to practice, prepare, concentrate. It's not easy when you don't know what's going to happen. " The Australian Open, nicknamed "The Merry Slam" by the players, is known for its high attendance and cheerful atmosphere. But the pandemic that still prevails could result in a very different tournament this year, with the possibility of lower attendance or even closed matches. Tennis Australia, the governing body of tennis in the country, has not yet made a final decision on the tournament. The governing body has said it is constantly observing the situation and that it is taking all necessary measures to ensure the health and well-being of everyone involved. Agassi concluded his interview with the hope that the situation would improve. "I love Melbourne and I love the Australian Open," he said. "I hope that this situation will improve and we can get back to the great sport that we all love." The tennis world will follow the situation in Melbourne with great interest. The Australian Open is not only the first Grand Slam of the year, but also sets the tone for the rest of the tennis season.
