In the novel ‘Engines of Creation’, author Peter H. Grehan uses the techniques of genetic engineering and robotics to present us with a future in which the art of genetic engineering is beginning to be understood. His main character, Dr. Alice Morrison, is a brilliant bio-engineer who has invented synthetic organisms called ‘bio-bots’, which can mimic human capabilities and augment human abilities. As the novel progresses, her bio-bots begin to have a much more active interaction with humans, attracting the attention of Mega-Corps, a powerful corporation with a dangerous and secret agenda. They are so keen to exploit the bio-bots that they attempt to abduct Alice and steal her technology. In an exciting twist to the plot, she strategically releases several improved bio-bots into society, which she is able to use to combat the corporation’s harmful plans. But as she does this, she has to face up to the moral ambiguity of her creations. She witnesses them struggle with their identity, becoming entangled in the grey area between artificial intelligence and human consciousness. One of the bio-bots, ‘Alpha’, experiences an existential crisis, which develops into a profound investigation into what it means to be human. And as the novel draws to a dramatic climax, Alpha brings together several other bio-bots to aid Alice in her fight against Mega-Corps. Their combined efforts lead to an epic showdown, in which the issues of autonomy, freedom and the ramifications of artificial intelligence are explored. At the end, the truth of the matter forces Alice to make a difficult decision, one which will have repercussions for the future of humankind. ‘Engines of Creation’ is an enthralling story that brings together elements of science fiction, ethics and philosophy. It poses thought-provoking questions about the distinction between human and artificial life and the consequences of crossing those boundaries.
