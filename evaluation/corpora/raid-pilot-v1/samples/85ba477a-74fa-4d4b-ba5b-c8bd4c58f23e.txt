The objective of this research is to use few labeled two-dimensional (2D) seismic slice data sets to train an attention-based convolutional neural network (CNN) on three-dimensional (3D) fault segmentations. In doing so, an end--end learning methodology with a modified encoder-decoder architecture has been adopted to classify 3D volumes based solely on limited input 2D labels while using spatial and temporal attention mechanisms to learn fault geometries across entire 3D space. Various evaluations have shown that our method can achieve comparable performance with larger labeled 2D datasets, even outperforming them in some cases, indicating the effectiveness of the proposed approach. Additionally, experiments from which CNN layer parameters were updated during training demonstrated superior performance over static models. Our results suggest that it may be feasible to develop effective 3D fault segmentation techniques utilizing small labeled datasets in a targeted manner, thereby reducing considerable costs associated with acquiring large labeled datasets and improving fault hazards mitigation assessments in oil and gas reservoirs.
