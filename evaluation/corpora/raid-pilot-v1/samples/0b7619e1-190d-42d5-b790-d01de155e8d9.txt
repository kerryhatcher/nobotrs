In a splendid display of endurance and skill, African athletes today swept to victory in both the men’s and women’s races in the Edinburgh Marathon. The man’s title was won by the Kenyan Elijah Kemboi in a time of two hours thirteen minutes and forty-six seconds, while his compatriot Caroline Jepchirchir took the women’s crown with a time of two hours thirty-two minutes and nine seconds. It is the first time in the history of the Edinburgh Marathon that both races have been won by African athletes. Kemboi, who was taking part in the event for the first time, was delighted with his performance. “The course was tough, but I enjoyed the run. The crowds were tremendous.” Jepchirchir, who had come second in last year’s race, said: “I am very pleased to have won. I trained hard for this race and I am glad that I was successful.” The Edinburgh Marathon is Scotland’s biggest sporting event and draws participants from around the world. This year, due to the severe Covid epidemic, all runners were required to wear masks before and after the race. Race Director, Dr Neil Kilgour, praised the performance of the African runners. “Elijah and Caroline both ran excellent races. Their victories show what a high standard of competition we have in the Edinburgh Marathon.” He went on to thank the runners and the people of Edinburgh for their support. “Despite the difficulties caused by the Covid pandemic, the Edinburgh Marathon has remained a great success and we are grateful to all those who contributed to the success of the event.” The Edinburgh Marathon, which was first run in 2003, is renowned for its flat, fast course. The race begins in the city centre and finishes on the picturesque East Lothian coastline. The double victory for African athletes is expected to enhance the profile of the Edinburgh Marathon, and encourage even more international runners to enter next year.
