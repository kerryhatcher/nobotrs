The story revolves around a young woman, Isabelle, who has been tasked with documenting the life and legacy of the historical figure Joan of Arc. As she delves into research, she begins to see her own experiences mirroring those of Joan’s. She starts questioning the boundaries between reality and myth, wondering how much of what we know about heroes like Joan is actually factual, or if they are simply exaggerations built by others.

As she explores these themes in her journal entries, which make up the bulk of the book, she slowly unravels the truth behind one of the most iconic figures in history. Along the way, she meets people from different backgrounds - scholars, soldiers, farmers, townspeople – all of whom hold their own version of Joan’s story. This allows her to gain insight into the complexities surrounding Joan's time in prison, trial, and death.

Throughout the narrative, Isabelle grapples with the weight of responsibility that comes with writing such an important work. She must remain objective while also sympathizing with a character whose bravery she admires greatly. In doing so, she learns valuable lessons about courage, perseverance, and sacrifice. 

As she concludes her task, she discovers a letter hidden in Joan’s personal documents. It reveals information that challenges everything she had previously thought. Shaken yet determined, Isabelle decides to share this newfound knowledge with the world.

"Joan's" final chapter leaves readers reflecting on the impact of individual choices in shaping society. Her journey serves as a reminder that our actions, no matter how significant or insignificant they may seem, can reverberate throughout history and influence generations.
