Former All Blacks captain Tana Umaga says he is ready and prepared for the challenge posed by the British and Irish Lions. The 42-year-old, who is currently the head coach of the Blues in Super Rugby, will face the Lions as they travel to New Zealand for their tour in June.

Umaga, who infamously tangled with Lions captain Brian O'Driscoll in the first test of the 2005 tour, insists he has moved on from the past and is looking forward to the competition.

"The past is the past, and I've put that to bed," said Umaga. "I'm focusing on getting the Blues ready for a tough encounter against a formidable Lions side. It's a new chapter and a completely different situation."

Umaga was involved in a controversial incident in the opening test of the Lions' last tour to New Zealand in 2005 when he and teammate Keven Mealamu were accused of spear-tackling Lions captain O'Driscoll. However, no action was taken against the players by the match citing commissioner.

The incident has been a talking point ahead of the Lions tour to New Zealand, but Umaga is eager to shift the focus towards the actual match.

Umaga admitted the Lions would pose a significant threat in the upcoming tour, but he was confident about the preparations being undertaken by his players. "We are up for the challenge, and we plan to give the Lions a run for their money. My team is motivated, skilled and ready for whatever the Lions have to throw at us."

Ahead of his side's meeting with the Lions, Umaga emphasized the significance of the event, stating how the Lions tours are always highly anticipated in New Zealand rugby and drive an unparalleled passion and enthusiasm among both the players and the fans.

While holding a profound respect for the Lions' prowess and legacy, Umaga asserted the readiness of his side to meet this challenge head-on, signalling that the Lions should expect nothing short of a fierce competition on the pitch.

"The Lions are an exceptional side with a rich history, but we are prepared and ready to welcome them to New Zealand," concluded Umaga. "This is shaping up to be one of the most exciting encounters in recent times. The stage is set for a titanic rugby showdown."
