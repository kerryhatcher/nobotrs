Scottish Premiership club Celtic FC has expressed dissatisfaction over the scheduling of its forthcoming European match against Bulgarian outfit Ludogorets in the Europa League.

The Bulgarian club had initially arranged the game to be played on 16th September, a date Celtic accuses of contravening UEFA regulations. The governing body's rules stipulate that no ties should be staged within a 24-hour period either side of the Champions League or UEFA League games without their authorisation. 

"We are unhappy about the proposed date. It clearly goes against UEFA's rules, and we are currently in talks with them to resolve the matter," said a spokesperson from Celtic. 

Celtic have been in contact with UEFA and requested the match to be rescheduled. The club believes the close scheduling of the games puts undue pressure on the players and potentially compromises the integrity of the game. 

Ludogorets, however, deny any wrongdoing, insisting that they liaised with UEFA ahead of confirming the game's date. "We followed all the necessary steps in planning the match and we have not received any objections from UEFA," responded a Ludogorets spokesman.

This is not the first time Celtic have raised concerns over fixture scheduling. Last season, the Scottish team lodged a similar complaint about another match being too close to a Champions League game.

As it stands, it remains uncertain if the match will go ahead as planned, or if UEFA will enforce a change. This brewing scheduling controversy adds an unexpected layer of tension to an already highly anticipated match.

Celtic are currently second in the Scottish Premiership standings, while Ludogorets top the Bulgarian First League. Like all football clubs in Europe, both teams are eager to remain competitive, maintain their physical health, and abide by UEFA regulations amidst a packed football calendar.
