An enthusiastic horticulturist, Simon Fletcher, has come out on top in a hotly contested national gardening competition. Known as an excellent designer and an experienced gardener, Fletcher’s attention to detail and flair for a striking display of design earned him the title of winner. However, the competition was a hard fought one, and Fletcher won by a mere five marks from his competitors. The National Gardening Competition, held at the Royal Horticultural Society in London, drew a record crowd to the prestigious event. The theme for this year’s competition was “Urban Greening”, intended to highlight the importance of green space for the health and well-being of city dwellers. Fletcher, a retired teacher of 58, used his six-by-five-metre garden to create a cityscape of eco-friendly, sustainable design, with small, recycled skyscrapers, green roofs and hanging baskets. He cleverly incorporated a miniature wildlife area into his design, and this won him marks for creativity and resourcefulness. The quality of his design was praised by both audience and fellow competitors. However, the runner-up, Alison Trent, a first-time competitor from Birmingham, was visibly disappointed. Her impressive design of a sustainable urban permaculture garden missed out on the top prize by a mere five marks. She said, “The competition was so close this year; Simon certainly deserved to win, but I’ll definitely be back next year.” After his narrow victory, Fletcher was impressed with the level of competition, and said he was proud to have won against such outstanding competitors from around the country. He said the event was a testament to the growing interest in gardening and to his belief in the importance of green space in cities. The competition ended with a gala dinner and prize-giving ceremony. The level of competition next year could be even more fierce, as Fletcher has vowed to defend his title.
