Feast of the Goat is a political novel set in the Dominican Republic during the last days of the Trujillo dictatorship. It is told from three points of view: Urania Cabral, a successful lawyer who returns to her country after a long exile; Antonio de la Maza, a conspirator in the plot against Trujillo; and, finally, the ruthless Trujillo himself. Urania Cabral reveals to us the personal horrors of Trujillo’s reign, the harrowing experience that drove her into self-imposed exile. The revealing confession of her father, a former senator, is at the heart of the harrowing experience she brings to light. In Antonio’s narrative, we see the dreadful story of the conspiracy to overthrow Trujillo, where the impetus for justice and vengeance is only equaled by the fear, the doubts, the potential consequences of their action. Trujillo’s perspective provides us with a chilling window into the mind of a dictator, showing us his obsession with power, his rage and terror, his desire for total control. The three narratives converge in a climax that ends with the assassination of the dictator. But it is the aftermath that gives us a clear picture of the impact of his regime and the aftermath of his actions on those who were involved. The Feast of the Goat is a bleak, haunting novel about power, corruption, and the moral complexity of resistance. It exposes the human cost of dictatorship, the lasting scars of its victims, but also offers the hope of justice and redemption.
