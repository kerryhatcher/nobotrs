This paper presents a novel approach for the detection of faults from microseismic data by means of an attention mechanism based on a sparse set of 2D ground truths. The major challenge in identifying faults is the high labeling cost and the large volume of the resulting 3-D dataset, which requires high inference speed. The proposed method, which uses the attention mechanism, reduces these difficulties, allowing for a better performance without the need for a ground truth. We show that the proposed method is efficient and effective by a series of experiments on several test data sets. We compare our method with the existing ones and show its superiority with respect to the precision, recall, and F1 score. Moreover, our method is trained with a sparse set of 2D ground truths, which demonstrates its strong learning ability with respect to sparse annotations and enhances its applicability in geological research.
