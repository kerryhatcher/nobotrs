His departure from Spain is not foreseen, says the British public. Kenyon said: “Arjen Robben is an important player and a valuable member of the squad, we have no intention of letting him go. We have ambitious plans for the future, and Arjen is an important part of those plans.” Robben, who moved to London from PSV in 2004, supposedly had a sour feeling about his situation. Kenyon, however, said that coach Jose Mourinho had great plans for Robben and that he saw him as an important cog in the machinery for next season. “We find him to be a unique asset to our squad,” Kenyon added. Barcelona, which is negotiating for a transfer to take the Dutch international to Spain, seems to have been denied a transfer by Kenyon's remark. The Catalan giants have succeeded in the past in recruiting a number of players from London, including the Icelandic striker Eidur Gudjohnsen. But now it seems that they will not be able to add Robben to their list of scouts. Unverified reports of discontent with Robben at Stamford Bridge have been circulating. Some think this may be due to the constant struggle with injuries in recent years. But neither the player, nor his agent, nor the club has made any statement on these rumors. Kenyon's words can put to rest all doubts about Robben's immediate future. But this is not the end of the rumors in football. The transfer window is still open, and the history of Robben and Barcelona may not yet be completely over.
