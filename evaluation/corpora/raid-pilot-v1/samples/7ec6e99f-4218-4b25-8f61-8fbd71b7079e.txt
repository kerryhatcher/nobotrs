In this academic paper, we propose a novel self-supervised clustering network architecture that utilizes spatial guidance to improve medical image segmentation. Our approach leverages both supervisory and unsupervised learning paradigms, employing the former to provide prior knowledge about tissue classes in images while utilizing the latter to identify regions with similar properties regardless of class. We design our system using an encoder-decoder backbone, which allows us to learn feature representations from raw image data and use them to automatically isolate various tissues within complex medical images. 

Our experiment results show improvement over previous state-of-the-art models across multiple evaluation metrics, demonstrating the effectiveness of our proposed methodology. The paper further discusses several potential avenues for future research, including improvements to preprocessing techniques, incorporating additional modalities into our framework, and investigating other applications beyond medical imaging. Overall, our work contributes significantly to the existing literature on medical image segmentation by introducing a powerful tool capable of accurately identifying and separating different types of tissue within complex medical images with minimal human input.
