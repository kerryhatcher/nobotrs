Ethiopia's Birhanu Legese and Kenya's Vivian Cheruiyot claimed victory in the men's and women's races respectively at the Edinburgh Marathon Festival. The event, which took place on Sunday, saw thousands of runners from around the world gather in the Scottish capital to take part in various races, including the marathon, half marathon, and 10k.

Legese, who finished in a time of 2 hours, 4 minutes, and 15 seconds, dominated the men's race from start to finish. The 26-year-old Ethiopian runner broke away from the pack early on and maintained a comfortable lead throughout the race. This victory marks Legese's second marathon win this year, following his triumph at the Tokyo Marathon in March.

In the women's race, Cheruiyot, a four-time Olympic medalist, crossed the finish line in a time of 2 hours, 18 minutes, and 31 seconds. The 36-year-old Kenyan runner, who is also the reigning London Marathon champion, displayed her exceptional endurance and speed as she pulled away from her competitors in the final stages of the race. Cheruiyot's victory in Edinburgh adds to her impressive list of accolades in the world of long-distance running.

The Edinburgh Marathon Festival is known for its scenic route, which takes participants through the city's historic streets and picturesque countryside. The event attracts both elite athletes and amateur runners, offering a unique opportunity for people of all abilities to challenge themselves and achieve personal goals.

This year's festival also featured a range of other races, including a team relay marathon, a junior race, and a wheelchair race. The inclusive nature of the event reflects the growing popularity of running as a sport and a means of promoting fitness and well-being.

The African dominance in the Edinburgh Marathon Festival is a testament to the region's rich running heritage and the incredible talent of its athletes. Ethiopian and Kenyan runners have consistently excelled in long-distance events, setting world records and winning major races around the globe.

As the Edinburgh Marathon Festival comes to a close, participants and spectators alike are left inspired by the remarkable performances of Legese and Cheruiyot.
