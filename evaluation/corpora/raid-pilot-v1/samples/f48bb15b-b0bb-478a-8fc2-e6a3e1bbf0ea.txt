The field of underwater sound technology has seen a significant evolution in recent years with research focused on using sonar imaging to increase our understanding of marine environments. This study presents an iterative, deep, and unsupervised synthetic aperture sonar (SAS) image segmentation methodology that allows accurate and efficient classification of seafloor topography, sediment texture, and other marine organisms within sonar images.

The proposed method is based on two key components: feature extraction followed by an iterative neural network approach to segmentation. Feature extraction involves extracting specific information from the sonar images, such as texture, edge strength, and intensity at various frequency bands. The resulting extracted features are then fed into an unsupervised autoencoder that learns to represent each area in a sonnet-like sequence. The output generated is then used in conjunction with an additional neural network architecture to perform multi-layer perceptron (MLP)-based synthesis to produce high-resolution segmented versions of the sonar images.

Experimental results indicate improved accuracy and efficiency compared to traditional methods. The iterative, deep, and unsupervised SAS image segmentation technique developed provides a useful tool for researchers working towards more effective monitoring and management of ocean habitats, allowing them to identify changes over time and detect critical indicators of ecosystem health.
