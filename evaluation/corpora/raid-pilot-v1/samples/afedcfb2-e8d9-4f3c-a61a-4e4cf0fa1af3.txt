A remarkable African double can be seen at the zoo in Edinburgh, Scotland.

Two baby lions have just been born to the mother lioness Kofi and her pride are overjoyed with the new additions to their family. The librarian and gardener at Royal Dick Springfield Park Zoo, said they were thrilled to announce the arrivals on social media. The animal kingdom is celebrating the births as the first black-and-white females ever seen at the Edinburgh zoo. The male child, who has already received his name, will carry on the rare genetic code that sets him apart from other lions with a white mane.

Kofi's brother Ndende was killed by poachers when he was just six months old. It is believed that the genetic mutation that causes white fur is more prevalent among wild lions, where it gives them an advantageous camouflage in the winter climate of Africa's savannah grasslands. In captivity though, this trait puts them at risk, making them stand out for predators. Despite this risk, there are only around 120 known black-and-whites in the wild, all living in Tanzania's Serengeti National Park. With the death of Ndende, Kofi became one the last surviving adult black-and-white males in the world until these two babies arrived days ago. This discovery could lead to further breeding and conservation efforts in Scotland, giving hope and light to the future of endangered species.
