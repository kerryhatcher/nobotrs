A fairy tale is being spun here on a backdrop of a Russian winter, in the last years of the century, and the hero is Anya, a poor peasant girl with a remarkable dancing talent. A story is unfolding in which Anya’s merry feet dance with abandon across the dazzling snows of her native village, and where she can enjoy the loveliness of nature. Then, a famous ballet master, who has come to the village from St Petersburg, takes a fancy to her capering feet and offers to give her his tutelage. Anya moves to St Petersburg and meets with harsh competition, her own personal torments, and the cold dissimilarity between the city and the country. During this time, her family is in danger of being evicted from their home. We follow the story of her progress in the city and of the family’s life in the village. In St Petersburg, she finds support and help in an unexpected friendship with Nikolai, a fellow dancer who instructs her in combining the simple, country-dance style with the steps of the classical ballet. Together with him, Anya finds her own individual style in the ballet world, with its characteristic dance across the snow. She comes to the climax when she is invited to dance in the imperial palace, where the story is brought full circle. As she dances, her own special way of dancing brings back the memory of the snows in her native village. The story of her ‘Dances on the Snow’ is a spellbinding portrayal of her inner torment and the determination to go ahead in spite of all obstacles. Her dance is accepted as a combination of classical ballet and the Russian folk dances, and it is pointed out that the hardships of the snowy village were the source of her early, primitive way of dancing.
