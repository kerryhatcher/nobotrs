Though tennis great Andre Agassi expressed his concern about the Australian Open, the escalating cases of Coxie 19 in Melbourne threatened to disrupt the event. On Tuesday, Agassi, who has won four of his eight Grand Slams in Melbourne, expressed his anxiety over the spread of the pandemic, the potential difficulties it presented to the tournament, and the people who would attend. I have many memories of the Australian Open, he said in a telephone interview. "The Australian Open is not only a competition, but also a celebration, and I'm afraid it's going to be different this year," he said. A series of restrictions has been imposed by the Australian government, including limits on the number of spectators allowed to attend outdoor sporting events. Such restrictions could affect the atmosphere of the tournament, which normally draws a large number of people. " Tennis thrives on the energy of the audience," said Agassi. When there are few, or perhaps no, spectators, the event will be altered. Agassi, who retired from tennis in 2006, also pointed to the difficulties of the compulsory quarantine of the players. "Keeping athletes in the hotel room for a week and a half and then asking them to perform at their best is going to be difficult." The Australian Open, scheduled to begin January 20, is one of the four annual Grand Slam tennis events. Although the tournament faces a number of challenges, despite these obstacles, the governing body has insisted that the event will take place. The world number one, Novak Djokovic, and women's champion Sofia Kenin recently joined a growing number of tennis stars who have expressed their concern about the uncertainties that the virus will bring.
