“The Child of the Wolves” is an interesting story about a boy who has been left in the wilderness of North America, in the wilderness of the North American interior, and who has been a long time without food, clothes, and drink. His parents, wolves’ researches, are tragically killed in a plane crash. He is alone and must find his own way in the wilds of North America. A pack of wolves finds him and instead of attacking him, they take him under their wing. The wolf chief, whom he names Shadow, becomes his father. The boy, Eli, learns to hunt, to communicate with the wolves and to survive in the wilderness, and grows to be like a wolf himself. As he gets older, he starts to doubt his identity and to wonder about his future and who he is, being both a wolf and a human being. His life takes another dramatic turn when hunters threaten his pack. He has to use his human wits and wolf instincts to protect his family. The story is a lyrical tale of survival and identity and the close ties between man and nature. The story also deals with family life, with friendship and with the primal instinct to survive. The story ends on a hopeful note. Eli has found a balance between his human and wolf instincts and has decided to live in harmony with nature as a true child of the wolves.
