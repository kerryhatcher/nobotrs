This novel is the story of a sensitive and literary youth growing up in a bourgeois family in Lübeck. It is the story of a young man's intellectual development and his struggle to reconcile his love of the arts with his complex feeling towards his middle-class upbringing. As the novel progresses, we see how he tries to overcome his sense of estrangement and the passionate longings for the beautiful and unattainable Ingeborg Holm and for his best friend, the friendly and artless Hans Hansen. Tonio's inner restlessness leads him to go abroad to Munich, where he studies art and literature. But his ambition to create something original is thwarted by his inability to escape his roots, and he is torn between his love of his home and his acceptance of his artistic inclinations. When he returns to Lübeck for a visit, he finds that despite his estrangement he still identifies with the people of his hometown and their simplicity. And at a carnival in Copenhagen, where he is mistaken for an artist, he finds himself, ironically, neither an artist nor a bourgeois. The novel culminates in a moment of self-realization, when Tonio recognizes that the art he loves is connected to the people whom he has rejected; and that he himself is bound, without understanding why, to both the art and the life which he once rejected. Through this inner journey the author paints a vivid portrait of the artist as a young man, as he attempts to negotiate the difficult terrain of self-identity, love and belonging.
