Tennis legend Andre Agassi has expressed concerns about the Australian Open as escalating Covid-19 cases in Melbourne threaten to disrupt the sports event. 

On Tuesday, Agassi, who won four of his eight Grand Slam titles in Melbourne, voiced his worry over the evolution of the pandemic and how it may affect the tournament considering the potential complications it poses.

"Melbourne is a very special place for me. I have numerous fond memories attached to it. The Australian Open is not just a competition, but a celebration, and I fear this year it may be very different," Agassi said in a phone interview. 

The Australian state of Victoria, home to Melbourne, has been struggling to contain a recent outbreak of the virus. As a result, the Australian government has introduced a range of restrictions, including a limit on the number of spectators that can be present at outdoor sporting events. This could greatly affect the atmosphere of the tournament that traditionally draws large attendance.

Agassi said, "Tennis thrives on the energy that the spectators bring. With limited fans, or perhaps no fans at all, it will surely impact the character of the competition."

The American great who retired in 2006 also commented on the significant toll the enforced quarantines could have on the players. "Forcing athletes to stay in hotel rooms for 14 days and then expect them to perform at the peak of their abilities will be challenging," he further added. 

The Australian Open, scheduled to begin in mid-January, is one of the four annual Grand Slam tennis events. Despite multiple logistical obstacles, tournament organizers have remained adamant that the tournament will go ahead.

Current world number one Novak Djokovic and women's champion Sofia Kenin recently joined the list of tennis stars to express their apprehensions about the complications the virus-induced protocols may bring.

As the global tennis community continues to grapple with the implications of the pandemic on their sport, the Australian Open's fate remains uncertain. It is a situation that everyone, including former champions like Andre Agassi, will be watching closely in the coming weeks.
