A charming romance which depicts the life of two sisters, Elinor and Marianne Dashwood, who are personifications of sense and sensibility. The story begins with the death of their father, which brings them into a strait, by the laws of inheritance which have given the estate to their brother John, while the former, being sensible, composed and rational, is prudent, measured and sensible, and the other, being emotional, excitable and romantic. The novel follows their course, as they struggle with the demands of social life, financial worries, and romantic intrigues. Elinor is in love with her brother-in-law Edward Ferrars, who is secretly betrothed to another woman, while Marianne is courted by the very agreeable, but inconstant John Willoughby, and also has the notice of the more reserved and older Colonel Brandon. The different characters of the sisters lead them into different ways; Elinor, by her sense, bears her heart-break with composure and dignity, but Marianne, by her sensibility, exposes herself to public humiliation and disgrace. The novel is filled with observations on the subject of love, the requirements of society, and the conflict between reason and sentiment. It points out the importance of a balance between the two; and in the end, Elinor marries Edward, who is released from his betrothal, and Marianne comes to appreciate the constancy of Colonel Brandon. The book ends with the sister united in marriage, each to the love of which they were entitled, and each possessed of the qualities which the other characterised.
