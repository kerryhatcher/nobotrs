Outstanding performances by African runners saw them clinch the men's and women's titles at the Edinburgh Marathon this weekend.

Ethiopian Hayle Lemi won the men's race clocking in at 2:10:33, while his compatriot Meseret Defar claimed the women's crown with an impressive 2:33:14. 

Lemi managed to hold off Kenyan Moses Koech in a close race, finishing just two minutes ahead after a nail-biting finale. The women's race saw Defar dominantly leading the pack, a clear five minutes ahead of her closest rival.

Thousands of spectators lined the city's streets, braving cloudy conditions to cheer on the athletes. Edinburgh’s enchanting landscape provided a scenic backdrop for the grueling 42.195km course, with both the men's and women's races setting off from the city centre and winding their way through the beautiful Scottish capital.

"What an amazing atmosphere, I am so proud to have won here and want to thank everyone who supported us along the way," said an elated Lemi after crossing the finishing line. Similar sentiments were echoed by Defar, who added, "It was a tough race, but Edinburgh's beautiful scenery helped me get to the finish line."

The Edinburgh Marathon, part of the Rock 'n' Roll Marathon Series, is known for its fast and flat route - making it a highly desirable race for both seasoned professionals and those looking to set personal bests. This year's event attracted more than 30,000 runners, making it one of the largest marathons in the UK.

Event organizers were highly impressed with the double African triumph. "We had a stunning field of athletes this year and we are happy to see the titles go to such deserving winners," said Event Director, Neil Kilgour.

In the half marathon event, another Kenyan, Eliud Kipchoge, won the men's race while Ethiopian Genzebe Dibaba triumphed in the women's race. The African dominance extended to the wheelchair race, with South African Ernst van Dyk winning the men's event, and Tatyana McFadden of the USA, originally born in Russia but adopted by an American woman, celebrated her victory in the women's category.

The extraordinary performances by the African athletes have once again showcased the continent's immense potential in long-distance running.
