The Umaga clan in Papua New Guinea is being prepared to welcome back their former chief and leader, who was sentenced to 27 years in prison on charges of murder and theft in 1984.

Local elders have set up a council meeting to decide how to receive Samoaan-born businessman Peter Omara, known in PNG by his local name, Chief Kaui Waulu, upon his release from prison Friday. The group has announced it will treat Omari as a hero upon his return, despite the accusations against him. He spent more than two decades behind bars for a number of crimes he denied committing, including killing a rival in Port Moresby in 1967 and theft.

Omari's departure from jail marks end of an era in PNG's criminal history, however some residents are concerned about his ability to control conflicts between rival factions within the community after spending time away from home. Nonetheless, Omaru's supporters in the country see him as an important figure who helped lift up many people through his wealth and influence during decades of crime and corruption in the country.

"He is going to reintegrate into society and we want to be there to support him," said one of Omaru's closest allies. "As such, it is our duty to show respect to him and show that we can work together."

The government in Papua New Guinea has expressed its hope that Omari will use his experience abroad to help rebuild the country and make it a better place for all citizens.
