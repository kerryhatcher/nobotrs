The Scottish football club Celtic has expressed its disappointment over the decision to move their upcoming Champions League match against Bulgarian side Levski Sofia to a neutral venue.

The match was originally scheduled to take place at Celtic Park in Glasgow, but UEFA has announced that it will be played at the Estadio Ramón Sánchez Pizjuán in Seville, Spain, on 27 September.

Celtic has cited safety concerns as the reason for its dissatisfaction with the move. The club has a history of playing in hostile environments in Bulgaria, and there have been incidents of violence and intimidation in the past.

"We are extremely disappointed that the match has been moved to a neutral venue," said Celtic chief executive Peter Lawwell. "We had made it clear to UEFA that we were not comfortable with the idea of playing in Bulgaria, given the history of incidents there.

"We understand the need for UEFA to make decisions based on safety concerns, but we believe that there are other ways to ensure the safety of players and fans without having to move the match to a neutral venue."

Celtic has also expressed its frustration with the timing of the move, which comes just days before the club's crucial domestic match against Rangers.

"We are particularly disappointed that the decision has been made so close to our domestic match against Rangers," added Lawwell. "This will cause significant disruption to our preparations and we are disappointed that UEFA has not taken this into account."

Despite its disappointment, Celtic has confirmed that it will comply with UEFA's decision and travel to Seville for the match.
