The story of Salamanca Tree Hiddle is a tragic narrative of a thirteen-year-old girl who travels with her eccentric grandparents across the country to her mother’s grave in Idaho. The narrative is a double narrative in which Sal tells the story of her friend Phoebe Winterbottom along with her own story, which is filled with mystery and wonder, but also resembles Sal’s life in many ways. Phoebe’s mother suddenly disappeared, just like Sal’s mother, who one day walked out on her and never came back. Sal, as she discovers more about the mystery of her friend’s life, begins to understand more clearly her own sense of loss, abandonment, and the complexities of human relationships. Meanwhile, her travels with her grandparents are full of humor, wisdom, and the charm of their eccentricities. They follow the path of Sal’s mother from Ohio to Idaho, and along the way, Sal learns about her Indian ancestry, the story of her parents’ love, and the events surrounding her mother’s death. It is a profound novel, full of grief, acceptance, and the power of storytelling. As Sal comes to terms with her mother’s death, she begins to walk two moons in the moccasins of others, to understand their feelings and points of view. The journey ends with Sal finding her resolution, understanding her mother’s choices, and learning to live her life without her.
