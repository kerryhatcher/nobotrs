Former All Blacks captain Tana Umaga has declared himself ready to face the British and Irish Lions in their upcoming tour of New Zealand. The 42-year-old, who is currently coaching the Blues in Super Rugby, has been working hard to prepare his team for the highly anticipated series.

Umaga, who famously clashed with Brian O'Driscoll during the Lions' last tour in 2005, said he was looking forward to the challenge. "It's always a special occasion when the Lions tour. They bring a lot of history and tradition with them, and it's a great opportunity for our players to test themselves against some of the best in the world," he said.

The Blues are set to face the Lions in the second match of their tour at Eden Park on 7 June. Umaga said his team had been focusing on their own game in preparation for the match, rather than worrying too much about what the Lions might bring.

"We've been working hard on our own game and making sure we're ready to perform at our best. We know the Lions will be a tough challenge, but we're confident in our abilities and we're looking forward to the match," he said.

Umaga also said he was excited about the prospect of facing the Lions as a coach, rather than a player. "It's a different challenge, but one I'm really looking forward to. I've learned a lot since my playing days and I'm excited to put that knowledge to use in this series," he said.

The Lions' tour of New Zealand will begin on 3 June with a match against the New Zealand Provincial Barbarians. They will also face the Crusaders, Highlanders, Maori All Blacks, Chiefs and Hurricanes, before the first Test against the All Blacks on 24 June.

Umaga said the tour would be a great spectacle for rugby fans. "The Lions only come around every 12 years, so it's a special occasion. I'm sure the fans will be in for a treat," he said.
