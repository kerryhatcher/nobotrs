In Sense and Sensibility, we follow the fate of the Dashwood sisters: Elinor, prudent and reserved; Marianne, eager and fanciful. After the death of their father, the sisters, with their mother and youngest sister, were forced into great poverty when their half-brother inherited the family estate. In this new situation, they have to face complicated relationships and their own feelings in order to find happiness and peace of mind. Elinor falls in love with Edward Ferrars, a gentleman who unfortunately, for the sake of duty, is secretly engaged to the sly and subtle Lucy Steele. On the other hand, Marianne passionately falls in love with the affable and frivolous John Willoughby, who falls in love with her more than once, to her great grief. Elinor tries to conceal her feelings and suffer in silence, and Marianne, who does not hide her feelings, is very sad when she is ignored by Willoughby. It becomes a turning point when Edward's secret engagement is revealed and he is disinherited. But it is the honor of his honor that wins Elinor's love and respect. On the other hand, the misfortune of Marianne is followed by the decline of her health, and she finally realizes the foolishness of her own emotions. As she recovers, Marianne feels safe and grows with Colonel Frank's steady love and comes to a mature understanding of love. At the end, both sisters, a person of sense and sensibility, come to their own. Edward breaks off his engagement to Lucy, marries Elinor, and lives happily as a petty husband. Marianne also gradually becomes calm and is finally able to accept Colonel Frank's proposal for her, appreciating his stable and noble love. Sense and sensibility depicts the fate of two very different characters who finally come to understand the importance of moderate emotion and the harmony of the two in love and in life.
