This paper describes CHASE, a new method of visual tracking which relies on cell-level differentiation of the neural architecture. The purpose of the work is to overcome the limitations of the existing methods which are often not sufficiently robust and not sufficiently adjustable because of their fixed architecture. CHASE introduces a new method which allows the tracker to adapt its architecture to different tracking tasks. The proposed method is evaluated on several benchmark data sets and shows a higher accuracy and robustness than the state-of-the-art trackers. These results suggest that cell-level differentiation of the neural architecture can effectively improve the adjustment and robustness of visual trackers.
