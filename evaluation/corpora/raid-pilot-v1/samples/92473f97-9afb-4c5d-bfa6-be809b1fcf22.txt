The task of segmenting objects within an image requires a comprehensive understanding of spatial relationships between different segments. Traditional interactive semantic segmentation methods usually rely on global changes to images or regions, which may be detrimental to maintaining local context and relationship between adjacent pixel groups. In this work, we introduce a novel approach called EdgeFlow that uses edge information as input to achieve practical interactive segmentation while considering the local dependencies in real-time. Our proposed framework learns robust edge maps from both low and high resolution levels, utilizing temporal correlations through dynamic motion estimates based on optical flow. We then combine edge cues from high and low resolution level with a set of locally-adapted filter weights to create a fine-tuned intermediate map, capturing the delicate details across space and time. Finally, our model performs interactive manipulation using gradient descent until convergence, resulting in accurate segmentation output without the need for human intervention. Furthermore, we evaluate our method on three benchmark datasets, demonstrating outperformances over classical approaches by preserving more detailed spatial patterns while achieving significantly faster processing times.
