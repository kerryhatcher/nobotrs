Former All Black captain Tana Umaga, now coach of the Blues, has announced he's ready for the British and Irish Lions on their upcoming tour of New Zealand. The 42-year-old, who has been coaching the Blues since the beginning of the year, said he was looking forward to the series. Umaga, who had a famous spat with Brian O'Driscoll on the Lions' tour of New Zealand in 2005, said he was looking forward to the challenge. "It's always a special occasion when the Lions come to town. They bring a lot of history and tradition, and it's a great opportunity for our players to test themselves against some of the best in the world," he said. The Blues will play the Lions in the second match of their tour, at Eden Park on June 7. Umaga said the Blues had been working on their own game in preparation for the Lions. "We've been working hard on our own game, and getting ready to play at our best. We know the Lions will be a tough challenge, but we're confident in our own ability and we're looking forward to the game." Umaga said he was looking forward to the challenge of facing the Lions as a coach rather than a player. "It's a different sort of challenge, but one that I'm looking forward to. Since I was playing I've learnt a lot, and I'm looking forward to putting that into the series," he said. The Lions' tour of New Zealand starts on June 3 with a match against the Provincial Barbarians, and includes games against the Blues, the Highlanders, the Maori All Blacks, the Chiefs and the Hurricanes before the first Test against the All Blacks on June 24. Umaga said the tour would be great for fans. "The Lions only come to town once every 12 years, so it's always a special occasion. I'm sure the fans are in for a real treat."
