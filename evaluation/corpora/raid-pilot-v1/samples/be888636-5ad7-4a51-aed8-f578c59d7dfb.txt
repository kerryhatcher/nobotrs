‘A Personal History of Joan of Arc’ re-enacts the historical drama of a legendary heroine through the eyes of a character from the past, the fictitious Monsieur de Conte. A childhood friend, Monsieur de Conte is the first to be aware of Joan’s intimate relations with the divine voices, which she confides to him and one other friend. The novel is set in the fifteenth century during the Hundred Years’ War, when Joan, a peasant girl, is inspired by a divine mission to liberate France from English occupation. With Sieur by her side she embarks on her mission, which defies the established order, promotes her to military commander and wins her enthusiastic followers. He describes her struggles, her agonies, and her victories, and his Joan is far more human than the legend that has been created of her. The admiration and love he feels for her shows how powerful a presence she was in the world. Despite her great successes, the intrigues and treacheries of the day lead to her capture and her trial for heresy and witchcraft, and Sieur’s masterly re-creation of these events gives a moving account of her courageous defence and her martyrdom. The novel concludes with Sieur reflecting on his life’s work of recording the history of Joan, which has kept her in the hearts of the nation and in history.
