The paper "CHASE: Robust Visual Tracking via Cell-Level Differentiable Neural Architecture Search" presents a novel approach to visual tracking that utilizes a differentiable neural architecture search (DNAS) method to optimize the tracking performance. The proposed method, called CHASE (Cell-Level Hierarchical Architecture Search for Efficient Tracking), uses a hierarchical architecture search strategy to identify the optimal architecture for visual tracking. The search is performed at the cell level, allowing for the exploration of a large search space and the identification of the most effective architectural components. The resulting architecture is then trained using a robust visual tracking dataset, resulting in improved tracking performance compared to state-of-the-art methods. The paper also presents an analysis of the effectiveness of the different architectural components and demonstrates the robustness of the proposed method to variations in the tracking environment. Overall, the paper demonstrates the potential of DNAS for improving the performance of visual tracking systems.
