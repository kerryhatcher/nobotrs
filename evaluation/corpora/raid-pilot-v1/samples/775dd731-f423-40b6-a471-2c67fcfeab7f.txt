Scottish champions Celtic have expressed their disappointment regarding the scheduling of their upcoming European tie against Bulgarian side Ludogorets Razgrad. The two-legged Champions League qualifier is set to take place in early August, which clashes with traditional celebrations of the Scottish festival of St. Mungo's Day. Celtic's Vice-Chairman, Dermot Desmond, has called for UEFA to reconsider the date in order to avoid disrespecting the club's rich heritage and religious customs.

St. Mungo's Day, also known as Glasgow's Patron Saint's Day, is observed annually on 13th of January in tribute to the city's founder, St Mungo. The festivities bring together Celtic fans, citizens of Glasgow, and people of Scottish heritage around the world. It is a time of celebration, cultural significance, and a display of pride in the club's roots.

Celtic's management team has protested the decision, citing the potential difficulties for supporters who want to attend the match whilst also honoring St Mungo's Day. The club is known for its strong fan base who are deeply connected to the club's history and traditions. Many supporters have voiced concerns over their inability to fully participate in the annual festival while also supporting their team in the crucial Champions League qualifier.

The significance of St Mungo's Day to Celtic fans is undeniable. The festival is not just a day of revelry; it is deeply ingrained in the club's identity. Celtic's origins can be traced back to the East End of Glasgow, an area with a historic association with St Mungo. The club's founders, Brother Walfrid and his associates, established the team in 1887 to raise funds for the charitable activities of the Catholic church. Thus, the club's history and their religious customs are intricately entwined.

In light of this, Celtic has requested that UEFA reconsiders the date of the fixture, allowing fans and players to fully participate in both the Champions League qualifiers and the St Mungo's Day celebrations. The club's Vice-Chairman, Dermot Desmond, hopes that UEFA will respect the club's long-standing traditions and find an alternative date that doesn't clash with such an important occasion.

Celtic's plight highlights the ongoing challenges faced by clubs as they juggle their sporting commitments with cultural and religious events. Balancing tradition and modern-day sporting demands can be a delicate task for clubs steeped in history.
