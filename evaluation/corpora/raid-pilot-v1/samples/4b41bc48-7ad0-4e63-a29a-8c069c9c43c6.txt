A major bridge in the Scottish Highlands is facing closure, potentially preventing it from serving pedestrians all this year.

The Kintyre Bridge on the west coast, built in 1879 and one of Scotland's busiest bridges over waterways, has fallen into disrepair due to high winds during storms earlier this year. The damage occurred in February when winds exceeding 60 miles per hour caused extensive damage to a critical section of the structure.

According to reports, engineers have found that several key components need to be replaced or repaired before the bridge can reopen safely to pedestrian traffic. This repair work is expected to take several months, which would effectively end its use for the remainder of the season.

Thousands of tourists flock to the area every year to discover the stunning scenery along the Kintyre and Cowal peninsula, the island which the bridge connects to the mainland. In addition to nature walks, cyclists also make heavy use of the bridge, as it offers scenic routes along the Gulf Stream Way cycle route.

Local shops, cafes, and restaurants cater specifically to these visitors, who come to enjoy the local fare and explore the nearby attractions such as Arran Golf Links, Loch Ness and Glencoe. Without the bridge, some businesses fear their revenue will drop significantly. However, the cost of repairs and potential closures are outweighed by the safety of passengers using the bridge, Transport Scotland said.
