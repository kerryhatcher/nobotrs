Former tennis champion Andre Agassi has expressed concern over the current air quality in Melbourne ahead of the Australian Open. Agassi, who won the Grand Slam tournament four times during his career, took to social media to voice his apprehension. The city has been grappling with hazardous levels of air pollution due to the ongoing wildfires that have been ravaging Australia.

Agassi, who now serves as a coach and mentor to several tennis players, raised doubts about whether it would be safe to play in such conditions. He emphasized the importance of the players' health and well-being, stating that the current air quality could potentially pose a significant risk to their respiratory systems.

The fires have caused smoke to blanket Melbourne and other parts of Australia, leading to a decline in air quality. Tennis Australia, the governing body of the Australian Open, has been closely monitoring the situation and has implemented measures to cope with the challenging circumstances. These measures include adjusting match schedules and potentially relocating matches to indoor courts with filtered air.

Agassi's concerns echo those of other tennis players, with several expressing their worries about the hazardous air quality. Dalila Jakupovic, a Slovenian tennis player, was forced to retire from her match during the Australian Open qualifiers due to breathing difficulties caused by the smoke. This incident shed light on the health risks associated with playing in such conditions.

Efforts are being made to alleviate the situation, including the installation of air purifiers on outdoor courts and the distribution of face masks to players and other personnel. Tennis Australia has also been working with medical experts to monitor the air quality levels constantly.

Despite these measures, some players remain skeptical about the feasibility of holding the tournament amidst the ongoing bushfire crisis. World number one, Rafael Nadal, expressed his concerns and stated that if the air quality did not improve, it would be difficult to go on with the tournament. He emphasized that the health of players, fans, and everyone involved should be the top priority.

Tennis officials are hopeful that the air quality will improve before the tournament begins. However, concerns over the potential health risks continue to loom, and the situation remains uncertain. With the start of the Australian Open just days away, all eyes are on Melbourne, waiting to see how the authorities handle this unprecedented challenge.
