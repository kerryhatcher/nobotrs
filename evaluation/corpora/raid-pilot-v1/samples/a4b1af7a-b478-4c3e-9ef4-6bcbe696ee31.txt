The story of this book is that of a long-abandoned child whose exile from her native land is intertwined with the story of the country. Urania Cabral, a successful lawyer, has returned after a period of thirty-five years to Santo Domingo, and finds her father’s house occupied by a sick old man, a fervent supporter of the evil regime. Her story then goes back in time, and traces the iniquities of Trujillo’s reign and the misfortunes that befell her personally, culminating in her own flight from the house as a child. At the same time, it brings to light the fear and repression that reigned in Trujillo’s kingdom. In parallel with it, Trujillo’s story reveals his cruelty and excesses, and gives the reader a terrifying view of his despotism. Although he is surrounded by his supporters, he is haunted by fears of an imminent assassination plot which is hatched by those who are close to him, thus proving his tendency to become paranoid and his unquestioning reliance on those close to him. A third thread deals with the secret plotters, and their struggle to overthrow the oppressive regime, their lives under the dictatorship, the devices they contrive and the inevitable collision with Trujillo, bringing the tension that has been building up to a dramatic climax. This book vividly depicts the corruption and despair of life under a dictator, the brutality of maintaining power, and the longing for liberation. The three strands of the narrative interweave and shed light on the whole of this period of the island’s history.
