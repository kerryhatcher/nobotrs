Double similarity distillation (DSD) is a novel neural network architecture that introduces two similarity measures, namely cosine and Euclidean distance, to improve semantically-based image segmentation. This approach combines both unsupervised contrastive learning and supervised training for better generalization in natural images with complex semantic structures. We analyze various experimental results, including average intersection over union (IoU), convergence rates, visual quality, and GPU efficiency, using multiple benchmark datasets such as LISAVY, MS COCO, PASCAL VOC, and Google Cloud Video Classification Dataset. Our findings indicate that DSD achieves state-of-the-art performance across standard evaluation metrics while consuming significantly less computational resources than other methods. Further analysis shows that our DSD model learns more generic features that can be applied universally to different types of objects across various applications at lower cost. Overall, this work demonstrates the effectiveness of DSD in achieving accurate object detection coupled with efficient computations on large diverse datasets, thus paving the way towards practical deployment.
