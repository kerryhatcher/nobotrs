Scottish football club Celtic have expressed their dissatisfaction over the scheduling of their upcoming UEFA Europa League qualifier against Bulgarian side Lokomotiv Plovdiv. Celtic manager Neil Lennon voiced his frustration, stating that the change in date was "unfair" and "disrespectful" to his team. He argued that the decision puts Celtic at a disadvantage, as they will have less time to recover and prepare for the match.

Lennon also highlighted the fact that the change in date was made without consulting Celtic, which he believes shows a lack of consideration for the club. He expressed his disappointment, stating that Celtic should have been given the opportunity to provide their input on the matter.

The Scottish champions are currently in the midst of a busy schedule, with domestic league matches and European qualifiers taking place in quick succession. The change in date for the Lokomotiv Plovdiv match adds further strain to an already demanding fixture list.

Celtic have been working hard to ensure they are in top form for the upcoming match, and the sudden change in schedule has disrupted their preparations. Lennon emphasized the importance of adequate rest and recovery for his players, and expressed concerns over the impact the change in date could have on their performance.

The club has reached out to UEFA to express their dissatisfaction and request a reconsideration of the scheduling decision. Celtic are hopeful that UEFA will take their concerns into account and make the necessary adjustments to ensure a fair and level playing field for both teams.
