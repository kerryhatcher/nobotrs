Former Manchester United star Ryan Giggs has been appointed the new manager of the Wales national football team, the Welsh Football Association confirmed on Monday, in what will be Giggs' first permanent managerial post. The man who won 64 caps for Wales during his glittering career as a player has signed a four-year contract. In taking over from Chris Coleman, who left in November to take over at Sunderland, Giggs, now 44, played for United all his career and won 13 Premier League titles, two Champions Leagues, four FA Cups and three League Cups. Giggs was also a caretaker manager at United for four matches at the end of the last season after the departure of David Moyes. "I am honoured to have been given the responsibility of managing the Wales national team," Giggs told a news conference. "The challenges that lie ahead in the Nations League and the qualification campaign for UEFA EURO 2020 are extremely exciting to me." Giggs' appointment has been received with a mixed reaction from Wales supporters. Some are delighted to have a player of his calibre at the helm while others have questioned his lack of experience as a manager. However, Giggs is confident he can handle the pressure. "I know there is a lot of work to do but I'm confident that with the support of the fans and the FA we can take Wales forward," he said. Giggs' first game in charge will be a friendly against China in March as part of the China Cup. His first competitive match will be in September when Wales play their first game in the new UEFA Nations League.
