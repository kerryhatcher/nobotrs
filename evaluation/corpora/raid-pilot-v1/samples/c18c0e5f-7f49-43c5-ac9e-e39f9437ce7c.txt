Juvenalia: An Ode to Natural Beauty

I stand before thee, Nature's majesty unfurled,
And marvel at thy wondrous splendor; behold!
Thy verdant canopies and azure skies,
Where clouds drift lazily like white-winged flies.

The sunlight filters through the leaves above,
A golden dance that warms my upturned love,
As shadows shift their shapes upon the ground,
In this cathedral where thy beauty is found.

From yonder stream, which sparkles in delight,
To mountaintops capped with snow so pure and white,
 Thou reignest over all, a ruler supreme,
Whose kingdom stretches across realms unseen.

Yet mortal hands have marred thy pristine grace,
With pollution's foul embrace. O wasted space!
How can we bear such careless disregard?
For every leaf, for e'ry humble bird,
Dost hold within its life an intricate art,
That mirrors our own fragile heart.

Oh let us pause, dear friends, to contemplate
The wonderous gift bestowed on this fair fate,
And pledge ourselves anew to Cherish, Preserve,
This treasure born of Earth's creative surge.
For as long as thou dost bloom so brightly there,
My soul shall find solace beyond compare.
