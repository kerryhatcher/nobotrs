The Calculus Affair unfolds against the gripping background of the Cold War. It is the story of a scientist, a professor of physics, who is kidnapped because he has worked out the formula for a revolutionary weapon: a sonorous machine which could raze whole cities. This coveted formula, written down in an enciphered notebook, is to be the focus of the rivalry of all the great world powers. The hero of the novel, a professional spy, Charles Lambert, has been sent to rescue the professor and recover the study. He has to travel from the tranquil streets of Geneva to the heart of the Iron Curtain, encountering a host of contrasting personalities: the brilliant biologist Helena, who will become Lambert’s indispensible ally, and the vicious double agent, Alexei, who is obsessed with obtaining the study. Starting from a subtly surveilled scene, and accelerating into the high-speed chase, the story develops a web of suspense, intrigue and scientific mystery. Throughout the race against time, Lambert and Helena are faced with the ethical dilemmas raised by the revolutionary potential of Professor Martin’s invention and the horrors it could unleash. In The Calculus Affair, the atmosphere of classic thrillers is suffused with the solemnity of scientific research. It is above all a poignant meditation on the thin line between the development of science and the onset of disaster.
