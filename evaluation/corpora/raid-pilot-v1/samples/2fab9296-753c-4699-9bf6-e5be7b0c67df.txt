The Africans took both the men's and the women's races at the Edinburgh Marathon on Saturday. Ethiopian Hayle Lemi won the men's race in 2:10.33, and his compatriot Meseret Defar took the women's title in 2:33:14. The winner, the two-time Olympian from the Horn of Africa, shaved a minute off the time of the winner of the last world cross-country run in 1959. Defar dominated the women's race and finished five minutes ahead of her nearest rival. The thousands of spectators lining the streets braved the dull weather to cheer the competitors as they struggled over the 42.195 kilometres course. “It's an incredible atmosphere, I'm very proud to have won here and I want to thank all the spectators,” said an exultant Lemi after crossing the line. Defar, too, praised the wonderful scenery on the course. The city marathon, part of the Rock and Roll series, is famous for its flat course, which makes it an ideal place for both professional athletes and amateurs looking to set personal bests. This year's event attracted more than 30,000 runners, making it one of the biggest in Britain. The organisers were thrilled to see the Africans take the titles. The Africans also dominated the half-marathon with Eliud Kipchoge of Kenya winning, and Genzebe Dibaba of Ethiopia taking the women's race. The wheelchair racers also went home victorious, with South Africa's Ernst van Dyk winning the men's event and American Tatyana McFadden, who was born in Russia but was adopted by an American, taking the women's title. The outstanding performances of the Africans once again demonstrated the immense strength of the continent over long distances.
