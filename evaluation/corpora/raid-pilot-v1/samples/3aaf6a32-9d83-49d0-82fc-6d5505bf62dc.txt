"Tonio Kroger" is a brilliant novel which depicts the life of a sensitive and intelligent young man of mixed German and Southern blood. It deals with his struggle to reconcile his artistic inclinations with the bourgeois principles which have been instilled in him from childhood. The novel begins with his childhood in a small town in Northern Germany where he feels a little out of place through his artistic tendencies and his mixed race. He is attracted to his schoolmates, Hans and Inge, who represent the carefree, blond and lusty German ideal which he can never attain. This unrequited love and his desire for acceptance and approval constitute the core of Tonio's early life. When he grows up he leaves his native town to follow his passion for writing. He becomes a successful writer in various European cities, but he is always tormented by his discontent, his inability to find a way of fully realizing his artistic vocation. In a scene which is pivotal to the novel he sees a group of dancers who remind him of Hans and Inge. This encounter brings about a deep self-awareness. He becomes aware of his profound longing for his past, his unfulfilled wish to belong to the bourgeoisie which he has rejected. The novel ends with his return to his native town where he finally confronts his feelings for Hans and Inge. He realizes that his love for them was not romantic but a longing to be like them. At the end of the novel Tonio accepts his double life as artist and bourgeois and finds peace in it. The novel is an exploration of identity and the dichotomy between art and life. It is a brilliant portrayal of an artist's search for his place in the world.
