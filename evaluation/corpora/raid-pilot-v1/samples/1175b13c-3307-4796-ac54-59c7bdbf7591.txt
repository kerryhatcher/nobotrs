Tana Umaga, former All Black captain and coach of the Blues, says he is ready for the challenge of the British and Irish Lions. Umaga, who is 42 and is currently coach of the Blues in Super 14, will take on the Lions when they come to New Zealand on their tour in June. He drew a little with Brian O'Driscoll in the first test in 2005, but now he says he has gotten over it and is looking forward to the challenge. “What’s past is past,” he says. “I’m just concentrating on getting the Blues ready for a difficult encounter with a strong Lions side. It’s a new chapter and a completely different situation. ” Umaga was involved in a controversial incident in the first test of the Lions’ tour to New Zealand in 2005. He and his teammate Keven Mealamu were accused of spearing the Lions’ captain, O’Driscoll, but no action was taken against the players by the referee in charge. However, the incident has remained in the headlines and has been discussed in advance of the Lions’ tour, but Umaga is keen to bring the talk back to the game. Umaga acknowledges the challenge the Lions will present but says his team is ready for them. “We’re ready for the challenge and we’ll do our best to beat the Lions. My team is motivated and we’re ready for whatever they can throw at us. When the Lions come to New Zealand it’s always a special occasion,” he says. “They’re the highlight of the season for New Zealand rugby and they’re always a huge talking point.” While recognising the Lions’ strength and history, Umaga says his team is ready to face the challenge and the Lions can expect a tough encounter. “It’s going to be one of the most exciting encounters between the two sides in recent years. It’s going to be a battle for supremacy on the field.”
