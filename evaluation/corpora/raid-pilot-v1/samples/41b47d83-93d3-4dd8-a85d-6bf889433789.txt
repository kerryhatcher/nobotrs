Ryan Giggs, the former Manchester United and Welsh International footballer, has been appointed as the lead coach of the Welsh national team. 

Giggs emerged as the first choice following a recruitment process conducted by the Football Association of Wales, with a panel led by its chief executive, Jonathan Ford. He will succeed Chris Coleman, who left the position to coach Sunderland in November. Giggs’ appointment has been described as a bold move by the association, with his playing reputation alone setting high expectations for the role. 

Having played for Manchester United from 1990 until his retirement in 2014, Giggs had an illustrious playing career, winning a staggering 13 Premier League titles and two Champions League trophies. This made him one of the most decorated players in football history. 

His decision to take up coaching has been praised by many ex-footballers and critics alike. "He has high tactical acumen and vast knowledge of the game that will be beneficial to the players", says former England international Michael Owen.

During his tenure as interim manager at Manchester United in 2014, Giggs showed strong potential and has since been an assistant manager under Louis van Gaal during his reign at United. However, this will be his first full managerial role and will undoubtedly bring new challenges.

The Welsh association has made it clear that they aim to qualify for the 2022 World Cup in Qatar, a competition that Wales has only once participated in back in 1958 when they reached the quarterfinals. In pursuing this goal, the association believes that Ryan Giggs is the right man to lead the team.

"The appointment of Giggs is a step in the right direction for Wales. His immense experience and impressive resume make him the perfect fit", comments BBC football analyst Ian Dennis. 

Wales, which boasts talent such as Real Madrid's Gareth Bale and Arsenal's Aaron Ramsey, finishes 2020 ranked 19th in the world, according to FIFA. Fans across Wales will hope that the appointment of a footballing legend like Giggs will help propel the team back into major tournament contention.
